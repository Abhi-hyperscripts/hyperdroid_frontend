CRM · Integration guide

Pipe Facebook Lead Ads into your CRM in under 30 minutes

A complete tenant-side walkthrough — from a fresh Meta account to lead rows landing in Ragenaizer CRM. We will build the Business Manager, run a real Lead Ads campaign, create your own Meta App, generate the System User token, paste it into the CRM, and connect a form. No code. No App Review. The integration belongs to you, not to us — the keyholder is your app, the token is your token.

~25–30 minutes first time
Re-use: ~1 min per future form
Cost: free (a small ad budget if you want a live campaign)
1
Welcome

What you will have at the end

Lead Ads on Facebook are powerful precisely because the user never leaves Facebook to fill out the form. Conversion rates are high; cost per lead is low. The catch — the leads sit inside Meta until somebody pulls them out. By default that means a marketing manager logging in to Ads Manager once a day, downloading a CSV, and forwarding it to sales. Half of those leads cool off before anyone calls them.

This guide wires the pipe end to end so that within two minutes of a prospect hitting Submit on your Lead Form, the lead row exists inside Ragenaizer CRM with its assigned source, owner, and field mappings — ready for the sales team to act on. No CSVs, no manual forwarding, no missed leads.

What you'll have working

A Facebook Lead Form on a Page you own, polled every two minutes by Ragenaizer CRM via a long-lived System User token issued by your Meta App. Every new submission becomes a CRM lead row automatically.

Architecture note

The reason this works without going through Meta App Review is that you create your own Meta App and issue the token from it. You are not asking Ragenaizer's app for any permission — you are handing Ragenaizer a token that your app already produced. Meta is fine with this, because the token never leaves the customer-of-record (you).

2
The 30,000-ft view

Six things, in this order

Before we touch a single screen it helps to see the whole shape. Six Meta-side artefacts plus one CRM-side connection. They have to exist in this order — you cannot skip ahead because each step references the one above it.

Business Manager │ │ owns ▼ Facebook Page ───────┐ │ │ │ runs │ assigned-to ▼ ▼ Lead Ad campaign System User (with Lead Form) │ │ generates ▼ Access Token (5 scopes) │ │ pasted into ▼ Ragenaizer CRM │ polls /forms + /leads ▼ Lead row in CRM

The piece that makes this whole flow work without Meta App Review is the System User. It is an automation identity inside your own Business Manager — not a person. The token it issues is long-lived, belongs to your app (not ours), and is scoped exactly to what the CRM needs to read leads. We never hold your password. We never see your Page. We only ever see the leads themselves, retrieved with the token you handed us.

Vocabulary

Business Manager — Meta's "control plane" for Pages, ad accounts, and people who can act on them. Page — the public face of your business; what runs the lead ads. Lead Form — the in-app questionnaire shown when a user taps the ad. System User — a non-human identity in the Business Manager that issues automation tokens. Scope — a single permission like leads_retrieval attached to the token.

4
Part A · Foundation

Set up your Business Manager

The Business Manager (Meta's newer surfaces call it a "Business Portfolio") is the container that owns your Page and your ad account. Without it, you cannot create a System User — and without a System User you cannot generate an automation token. So this is step one.

Create the Business Portfolio

Go to business.facebook.com and click Create. You'll be asked for three things: business name, your name, and your email.

Naming gotcha

If Meta rejects your name with "The name you chose for this Business Manager is not valid", it is matching a substring in a blocklist (often a trademark). Try a longer name — e.g. add "Technologies" or "Pvt Ltd" — or pick a name that doesn't share a word with a known brand. The rejection is not appeal-able.

Confirm the email Meta sends you. The portfolio is now live but empty.

Add your Facebook Page

Settings → Accounts → Pages → Add → Add a Page. Type the page name (or paste its URL) and pick the right one from the suggestions.

Add an ad account

Settings → Accounts → Ad accounts → Add → Create a new ad account. The ad account is technically optional for retrieving leads — the SU token reads leads from the Page, not the ad account — but Meta's UI gates several Lead Form pages behind one being attached to the portfolio, so it's easier to create one now.

Done with Part A when

You can see your Page and your ad account both listed inside the Business Portfolio Settings. We will reference both later from the System User.

5
Part B · The actual ad

Create your Lead Ads campaign (optional)

This part is optional. The integration works fine without ever publishing a paid ad — we will create the Lead Form directly via the Graph API in Part F, and you can fire test submissions at it through Meta's Lead Ads Testing Tool. If you only want the pipe to work, skip to Part C now.

If you want to actually run a campaign so real users see your ad, read on. The walkthrough below shows the full flow inside Ads Manager from empty campaign to draft state. Whether you publish (and therefore charge your card) is up to you — Meta lets you save the entire campaign as a draft for as long as you want.

Pick the Leads objective

Open Ads Manager for your ad account. Click + Create, then pick the Leads objective.

Configure the campaign

Name it something descriptive (we used "Demo Lead Capture — Ragenaizer"). Keep the default budget low — you can lift it later once you see leads flowing.

Accept Lead Generation Terms (one-time, per Page)

When you reach the Ad Set step and pick your Page, Meta will block you with this banner:

Configure the audience

The Ad Set page is where you pick the country, age range, language, and optional interest targeting. For a test we leave most defaults — Meta auto-fills a broad audience.

Build the Lead Form

Continue to the Ad step, scroll to Destination → Instant form, click Create a form, and pick Create from scratch.

1. Form type

Pick More volume (the default). It is the friction-free path that gets the highest fill rate. "Higher intent" adds a confirmation step that drops conversion by ~30%.

2. Intro

Headline (max 60 chars) and a short description. This is what the user sees before they start filling.

3. Questions

The default contact-info questions (Email, Full name, Phone number) are exactly what Ragenaizer CRM expects out of the box. Leave them as-is unless you have a reason to change.

4. Privacy policy

Required. Paste your public privacy-policy URL.

5. Ending

Title + description for the thank-you screen, plus a follow-up action. Picking Go to website with your homepage URL is the most common choice.

Forms become immutable on publish

This is a common surprise. Once a Lead Form is published, Meta locks every field, every question, and the privacy URL. The reason is consent integrity — every lead collected against the form must carry the exact schema the user agreed to. If you find a typo, your only option is to duplicate, fix, publish the new one, attach it to the ad, and archive the old one.

Save as draft

Click Save draft at the bottom-left of the form builder. The form is now saved inside your draft ad campaign. The campaign list will show three drafts (campaign, ad set, ad).

Why we don't publish in the guide

A Lead Form saved as a draft inside an Ad Manager campaign is not visible to the Graph API — meaning Ragenaizer CRM will not see it either. We solve this in Part F by creating a separate, instantly-published Lead Form via the Graph API directly. This costs nothing and gives you a working form ID immediately. The draft form above can stay as your in-progress paid campaign whenever you choose to publish it.

Part C · The keyholder

Create your own Meta App

Every API token Meta issues is bound to a specific app. So you need an app of your own to be the keyholder for your token. This is not the Ragenaizer app — it lives in your Business Manager and you control it. You will not be submitting it for App Review; it just exists to issue you one long-lived System User token.

Register as a Meta Developer

Go to developers.facebook.com. If this is your first time, you'll see Get Started. Click it.

Verify your account

Meta requires either a phone number or a credit card before you can create any app. Phone is faster.

Confirm contact email + role

Email is pre-filled from your Facebook profile. Pick Owner / founder (or whatever describes you) and click Complete Registration.

Create the app

You land on the empty My Apps page. Click Create App.

App details

Name it descriptively (e.g. "Acme CRM Integration"). 30 character max. Email is pre-filled.

Pick the first use case

Meta's app creator forces you to pick exactly one "use case" up front. Pick Create & manage ads with Marketing API.

Connect to your Business Portfolio

The Business step asks which portfolio should own the app. Pick the one you created in Part A.

Skip the Requirements step (no requirements yet), review the Overview, click Create app, and re-enter your password when Meta asks.

Add the second use case — Capture & manage ad leads

This is the step nobody realises they need until their token fails to pull leads. The default Marketing API use case grants pages_manage_ads, ads_read, business_management — but not leads_retrieval. You have to add a second use case to unlock it.

Sidebar → Use cases → + Add use cases (top-right). Click the Ads and monetization filter to narrow down.

Don't skip this

If you only have the first use case, the System User token you generate in Part D will silently not include leads_retrieval. The CRM will accept the token, find no forms, and you'll spend an hour wondering why — only to come back here.

Confirm your App ID

Sidebar → App settings → Basic. Note your App ID. You don't need to copy the secret — it doesn't get used by this integration.

Part D · The token

System User & access token

This is the most important section of the guide. We're about to create the non-human identity that issues your long-lived API token, attach it to your Page and your App, and generate the token itself. Read carefully — one specific click order matters.

Create the System User

Go to Business Manager → Settings → Users → System users. Click + Add.

Assign your Page

The SU now exists but has no access to anything. Click Assign assets, switch to Facebook Pages, tick your page, and toggle Full control → Everything on the right.

Click Assign assets at the bottom of the dialog. You should see "Assets assigned. 1 asset was assigned".

Install the App for the System User

The SU also needs to be installed on your Meta App. Go to Settings → Accounts → Apps, click your app, click Assign people, tick the System User, and toggle Manage app (Full control) on the right.

Generate the token

Back to Settings → System users, click your SU, then click Generate token at the top right. The wizard has four steps.

1. Select app

Pick the Meta App you created in Part C from the dropdown.

2. Set expiration

Choose Never. The default 60-day token expires and silently breaks lead capture every two months. Long-lived tokens are safe because they are tied to your specific Page + App + scopes.

3. Assign permissions — the critical click

This is the step that trips everybody up. The dropdown lists ten permissions. You need five of them. Click only leads_retrieval.

Click only one. Do not click the others.

When you click leads_retrieval, Meta auto-locks five required dependencies in (you'll see them turn into disabled-but-checked rows): business_management, pages_manage_ads, pages_show_list, pages_manage_metadata, and pages_read_engagement. The dropdown header reads "6 options selected".

If you click any of the others first, Meta will silently drop leads_retrieval from the issued token even though it still appears selected in the UI. This is the #1 reason tokens fail validation later.

4. Done — copy the token

Verify your token (optional)

If you want to confirm the scopes before pasting it into the CRM, run this from your terminal:

curl "https://graph.facebook.com/v23.0/me/permissions?access_token=YOUR_TOKEN"

You should see seven entries, all status granted: leads_retrieval, business_management, pages_manage_ads, pages_show_list, pages_manage_metadata, pages_read_engagement, public_profile. If leads_retrieval is missing — revoke the token, go back to the Meta App and confirm the second use case ("Capture & manage ad leads") is saved (Part C), then re-issue clicking only leads_retrieval first.

8
Part E · CRM-side

Paste the token into Ragenaizer

The Meta side is done. Everything from here is one screen.

Open Integrations

Log in to Ragenaizer, open CRM, then Settings → Integrations.

Paste & validate

The CRM hits Meta's /me/permissions endpoint, confirms every scope is present, then fetches every Page your SU has access to.

Tick the Page(s) you want to connect, click Connect Selected.

What just happened

The CRM stored your token (encrypted at rest), exchanged it for a per-Page access token, and recorded the page metadata. Polling for forms and leads will now happen every two minutes via Hangfire on our side — you don't need to do anything to keep it running.

Part F · Form mapping

Connect a Lead Form & map fields

Each Page can host many Lead Forms. You decide which ones the CRM should pull from, and how their questions map onto CRM lead fields. Both decisions happen in one screen.

Open Manage Forms

On the connected page card, click Manage Forms.

If your form list is empty even though you built one in Part B, that's expected: forms saved as drafts inside an Ad Manager campaign are invisible to Meta's Graph API until the campaign is published. The cleanest fix is to create the form via the API directly. It takes one curl call, costs nothing, and gives you a working ACTIVE form id.

Create a Lead Form via API (no ad spend)

From your terminal — replace {TOKEN} with your SU token from Part D and {PAGE_ID} with your Page ID (visible in the connected-page card or under About on your Facebook Page):

PAGE_TOKEN=$(curl -s "https://graph.facebook.com/v23.0/{PAGE_ID}?fields=access_token&access_token={TOKEN}" \
  | python3 -c "import sys,json; print(json.load(sys.stdin)['access_token'])")

curl -X POST "https://graph.facebook.com/v23.0/{PAGE_ID}/leadgen_forms" \
  -d "access_token=${PAGE_TOKEN}" \
  --data-urlencode 'name=Demo Lead Form' \
  --data-urlencode 'follow_up_action_url=https://example.com' \
  --data-urlencode 'privacy_policy={"url":"https://example.com/privacy","link_text":"Privacy Policy"}' \
  --data-urlencode 'questions=[{"type":"FULL_NAME"},{"type":"EMAIL"},{"type":"PHONE"}]' \
  --data-urlencode 'thank_you_page={"title":"Thanks","body":"All set","button_text":"View website","button_type":"VIEW_WEBSITE","website_url":"https://example.com"}' \
  --data-urlencode 'locale=en_US'
# → {"id":"NEW_FORM_ID"}

The form is created with status ACTIVE immediately. No payment method, no ad spend, fully queryable. Reload the Manage Forms dialog and your form will appear.

Map the questions to CRM fields

The Label this source field at the top determines what shows up in the Leads page filter, so name it however your sales team thinks about it (e.g. "Q4 Webinar Form" or "Diwali Promo"). Click Save Mapping.

Verify the form is polling

Done. The pipe is live.

From here on, any submission against your form — whether from a real ad, from Meta's Lead Ads Testing Tool, or from a published version of the campaign you drafted in Part B — will land as a CRM lead row within two minutes, with the source label, owner, and field mappings you configured here.

If something didn't work

Troubleshooting

The four most common ways this integration fails — and how to recognise them quickly.

Token is valid but no forms show up

Your forms are in DRAFT state inside an Ad Manager campaign. Meta's Graph API does not return draft forms even when you ask explicitly. Either publish the campaign (charges your card) or create the form via the Graph API as shown in Part F.

"This token does not include leads_retrieval"

Two possible causes:

  • The second use case ("Capture & manage ad leads with Marketing API") wasn't added to your Meta App. Go back to Part C, add it, revoke the bad token, regenerate.
  • You clicked permissions individually instead of clicking only leads_retrieval. The dropdown silently drops it from the issued token. Revoke and regenerate clicking only leads_retrieval first.

"No permissions available" in the token wizard

The System User isn't installed on the App. Go to Business Manager Settings → Accounts → Apps → click your app → Assign people → tick the SU → toggle Manage app. Then re-run Generate token.

Ads Manager refuses to publish your ad with "Terms of Service Not Accepted"

Every Page must accept Lead Generation Terms once before it can ever run a Lead Ad. Click View terms in the red error and agree. One-time per Page, lasts forever.

Business Manager won't accept your name

Meta has an opaque blocklist for substrings that look like trademarks. Add a generic suffix (e.g. "Technologies", "Pvt Ltd"), or pick a name that doesn't share a word with a known brand. There is no appeal.

Form was published with a typo

Lead Forms are immutable on publish. Click the form → Duplicate, fix the copy on the duplicate, publish the new one, swap it into the ad, archive the old one. There is no edit.

Token works for a while, then stops

Almost always one of:

  • You generated the token with a 60-day expiry instead of Never. Re-issue with Never.
  • The Page admin who set up the Lead Generation TOS got removed from the Page. Re-accept the TOS as a current admin.
  • The System User itself was deleted. Recreate it (or pick another SU, but you'll need to re-issue the token).

Glossary

TermWhat it means
Business Manager (Business Portfolio)Meta's container for Pages, ad accounts, people, and apps. The administrative root of everything in this guide.
PageThe public Facebook Page that runs Lead Ads. Lives inside a Business Manager.
Ad accountThe billing-and-campaigns container under a Business Manager. Country, currency, and time zone are locked for life once set.
Lead AdA Facebook ad whose CTA opens an instant in-app form rather than sending the user to a website.
Lead Form (Instant Form)The questionnaire shown when the user taps a Lead Ad. Owned by a Page, immutable once published, queryable via Graph API as /{page_id}/leadgen_forms.
Use caseA Meta-defined bundle of permissions you attach to your app. This integration needs two: Create & manage ads with Marketing API and Capture & manage ad leads with Marketing API.
System User (SU)A non-human identity inside a Business Manager. Issues long-lived API tokens for automation. Cannot log in to facebook.com.
Scope / PermissionA single capability (e.g. leads_retrieval) attached to an access token. The five scopes this integration uses are listed in Part D.
Access TokenA long string starting with EAA... that authenticates Graph API calls. The one we generate is permanent (Never expires) and bound to your Page + your App + your SU.
Graph APIMeta's REST-ish API at graph.facebook.com. The CRM polls two endpoints: /{page_id}/leadgen_forms for the list of forms, and /{form_id}/leads for new submissions.
HangfireThe Ragenaizer-side scheduler that runs the two-minute polling job. You do not configure it — it just runs.
Lead Ads Testing ToolMeta's free tool at developers.facebook.com/tools/lead-ads-testing that lets you fire test submissions at any of your Lead Forms without paying for an ad. Useful to verify the pipe end to end.
