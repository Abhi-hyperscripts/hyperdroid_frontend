CRM · Implementation guide

How to set up CRM — from first prospect to first invoice

A complete walkthrough of Ragenaizer CRM. We will build a real sales pipeline from a clean install: configure stages and lead sources, capture a company, attach contacts, run a deal through the kanban, and watch what happens the moment that deal closes Won — the company is auto-promoted to an Accounts customer for billing, with the same UUID flowing across services.

~15 minutes end to end
Tenant: demo
Backends: CRM 5112 · Accounts 5122
1
Welcome

Why a CRM, and what makes this one different

A CRM is the home of your sales pipeline. It captures the people and organisations you are talking to, the conversations you have with them, and the open opportunities you are working to close. Done well, it answers the only two questions sales leadership ever really has — who’s in the funnel, and what’s going to close this quarter.

Ragenaizer CRM looks like the CRMs you already know — Pipeline, Kanban, Companies, Contacts. The difference is what happens at the end of the line. When a deal moves to Won, the CRM does not stop at “congratulations”. It hands the won counterparty over to Accounts as a client_vendor_request for Finance to review. On approval the company appears as a customer in your books, with the same UUID, ready for invoicing. There is no copy/paste between systems and no “Sales said this customer exists; Finance has no record” drift.

Vocabulary

Prospect — a company in the CRM that has not yet closed a deal. Lives only in CRM. Customer — same company after a deal Won; now also exists in Accounts under the same id. Promotion — the cross-service event that creates the Accounts customer record from the CRM company on Won.

2
The 30,000-ft view

Pipeline is wide, books are narrow

Sales adds dozens of companies and contacts a day. The vast majority will never become customers. If every one of them hit the Accounts approval queue, Finance would drown in noise about prospects they will never invoice. So we draw the line in the right place:

CRM (wide) ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ Accounts (narrow) Companies, Contacts, Leads, Deals Customers, Invoices, GL live freely. No approval required. Strict. Auditable. Status = "prospect" by default. Each row touched money. ┌──────────────────────┐ ┌──────────────────────┐ │ Northwind (Prospect)│ │ │ │ ↓ │ │ (empty) │ │ Deal $480K │ │ │ │ ↓ stages │ │ │ │ Won ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ promote ━━━━━━━▶ client_vendor_req │ │ │ (same UUID) │ ↓ Finance approves │ │ Status flips to ◀━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━ │ customer CUST-0001 │ │ "Customer" (green) │ │ ready for invoices │ └──────────────────────┘ └──────────────────────┘

The thing that makes this work is the shared UUID. When a CRM company is promoted on Deal Won, CRM passes its company.id along with the request. Accounts uses that same id for the request and (on approval) the resulting customer row. From that moment on, deals, proformas and invoices in Accounts can all FK back to the same identity the salesperson saw in CRM.

3
Session A

Login & the CRM dashboard

From the Ragenaizer home grid, click into CRM. Every module on the home page is the same shape — pick yours and go.

The CRM dashboard opens to a clean board: four KPI tiles at the top (Total Leads, Active Deals, Pipeline Value, Converted Leads), a lead funnel, and a row of Quick Action cards.

Tip

Numbers on the dashboard are read-only and refreshed live. If a tile shows zero when you expect data, hit the Refresh button top-right instead of reloading the page — faster and avoids losing any unsaved work in another tab.

4
Session B · setup foundation

Settings first — the pipeline that everything else hangs from

Resist the urge to jump into Companies. Take five minutes in Settings → Pipeline first. The deal stages you define here are referenced from every deal, every kanban column, and from the cross-service promotion logic (which fires only when a deal lands in a stage of type won). Adding a stage later is fine; deciding what they should be is the easy thing to skip.

4.1 General

The General tab holds the default currency for new deals. Set it to whatever your books are kept in. We leave it at USD ($) for the demo.

4.2 Pipeline stages

A fresh tenant has zero stages. The kanban won’t render anything until you create at least one Open stage and at least one Won/Lost stage.

For the demo we click Seed Defaults, which gives us Qualification → Proposal → Negotiation → Won → Lost. Each stage carries a stage_type (open / won / lost), a win-probability percent used in forecast totals, and a colour for the kanban header.

Why the type matters more than the name

You can call a stage anything you want — “Closed Sale”, “Booked”, “PO Received”. But the promotion to Accounts only fires when the stage’s stage_type is won. If you create a custom “Verbally Agreed” stage and forget to set its type, dropping a deal into it will look like a win in the kanban but Finance will never hear about it.

4.3 Lead Sources — how leads will arrive

Switch to the Lead Sources tab in the sidebar. A lead source is a configured channel through which leads enter your pipeline: a contact form on your marketing site, a Google Ads landing page, a LinkedIn lead-gen ad, an event scanner app, or just “Manual” for leads someone types in by hand. Each source gets its own webhook URL, so you can wire any external system to drop leads straight into CRM.

Click + Add Lead Source to open the modal.

Field mappings tell CRM which incoming JSON field names to interpret as which CRM fields. The defaults cover almost every form library on the planet (first_name, firstName, fname, given_name all map to First Name; same shape for Email, Phone, Company, Job Title, etc.). If your source uses an oddball field name, add it to the comma-separated list and CRM will pick it up.

For our demo we’ll create a simple Website Contact Form source:

  • Source Name: Website Contact Form
  • Source Type: Website
  • Source Identifier: northwind-traders.example.com
  • Field Mappings: leave defaults

The new source lands in the table with six row actions: Copy URL (the webhook endpoint), Customize Form (the live-preview form designer), Embed Code (snippet to paste on your site), Edit, Regenerate Webhook Key, Delete.

4.4 The Embed Code — two ways to paste it on your site

Click the Embed Code action on the row. The dialog gives you two options — the choice depends on how much control you want.

4.5 Form Styling — the live-preview designer

Click the Customize Form action (paintbrush icon) on the source row. You land in a full WYSIWYG form designer.

Every change re-renders the preview instantly. The controls cover:

GroupKnobs
LayoutForm Title, Theme (Light/Dark), Position (Center/corners), Show field labels
ColorsBackground, Opacity, Text, Label, Input fill, Input text
SizingForm width (desktop), Input height, Button height
ButtonBackground colour, text colour, label text
BrandingLogo URL, position (header/footer), height
ShapeBorder colour, corner radius, frosted-glass effect toggle
Workflow

Style the form once per source. Click Save Styling when you’re done — the Widget Script in §4.4 will render the pop-up with these settings on your live site without you touching the embed code again.

End of Settings — what's in the database

One row in deal_stages per pipeline stage. One row in lead_sources with a unique webhook_key for inbound auth. The styling JSON lives on the lead-source row itself (field_mappings + a styling blob). Nothing else has touched your books yet.

Session C

Companies — the prospects in your pipeline

A Company in CRM is any organisation you might one day sell to. It is intentionally cheap to add: name, email, phone, address, and an industry tag. No GST number, no payment terms, no credit limit — those are Finance’s concerns, asked for only at promotion time when it actually matters.

Click Add Company and the modal slides in.

Filling it in for our demo customer:

  • Company Name: Northwind Traders Pvt Ltd
  • Industry: Retail
  • Website: https://northwind-traders.example.com
  • Phone: +91-9988776655
  • Email: accounts@northwind-traders.example.com
  • Address: Unit 304, Tower B, DLF Cyber City
  • City / State / Country: Gurugram, Haryana, India

Click Create Company. The row appears immediately in the list with a neutral PROSPECT badge.

What just happened in the database

One INSERT into the local CRM companies table. Zero gRPC calls to Accounts. The CRM company exists as a sales-pipeline record only — it has no presence in your books yet, and won’t until a deal closes Won.

6
Session D

Contacts — the people you actually talk to

A Company is an abstraction; deals are won and lost by the humans inside it. A Contact in CRM is one person, optionally attached to one Company. The same person can switch employers without you losing their history — reassign the company link and all their past leads, deals, and notes follow.

Open the Contacts page, click + Add Contact, and fill the form pointing at our Northwind company:

  • First / Last Name: Pradeep Kapoor
  • Email: pradeep.kapoor@northwind-traders.example.com
  • Phone / Mobile: +91-1244567890 / +91-9988776654
  • Company: Northwind Traders Pvt Ltd (picked from the dropdown)
  • Job Title: Head of Procurement
  • Contact Source: Website

Save and the contact lands in the list, joined to its company.

Sales tip

Always fill Job Title. Knowing whether you’re talking to the Head of Procurement, the CTO, or the office manager changes the entire deal motion. Reports later let you slice pipeline by title.

7
Session E

Leads — the top of the funnel

A Lead is a person who has expressed some interest but hasn’t been qualified yet. They might be a form fill from your website, a name from a conference, a referral from a partner. Leads have their own status track (new → contacted → qualified → converted | unqualified) so you can see at a glance how the top of your funnel is moving.

Click + New Lead on the Leads page and capture an inbound:

  • Name: Anita Verma
  • Email / Phone: anita.verma@kovid-foods.example.com / +91-9123456789
  • Company / Title: Kovid Foods Pvt Ltd, Operations Director
  • Lead Source: Website (matches a source we configured in §4)
  • Notes: Inbound from website contact form. Looking at our SaaS pricing for a 200-person team. Wants a demo next week.

The lead lands in the table with status NEW. Once you have spoken to them and qualified the opportunity, the row gets a Convert to Contact action that promotes them out of the lead funnel and (optionally) creates a Deal in one shot.

Lead vs Contact vs Company

A Lead is unverified interest — we haven’t decided yet whether they belong in our long-term contact list. A Contact is a verified person we maintain a relationship with. A Company is the organisation behind that contact. Leads convert into a Contact (and usually a Deal) once you decide they’re real.

8
Session F

Deals — money in motion

A Deal is the actual revenue opportunity. Deal Name, Deal Value, Currency, Stage, Expected Close Date, and (most importantly) the Company it is for. Open the Deals page and you land on the Kanban view: one column per pipeline stage, drag a card across columns to move it through the funnel.

Click + New Deal and create one for Northwind:

  • Deal Name: Northwind — Annual SaaS Subscription
  • Deal Value: 480000 (in the currency from §4.1)
  • Stage: Proposal (we’re past initial qualification)
  • Expected Close Date: 2026-05-15
  • Company: Northwind Traders Pvt Ltd (picked from the dropdown)
  • Notes: 3-year contract, 200 seats, dedicated CSM. Closing this quarter.

Save and the card lands in the Proposal column.

Drag the card from Proposal into Negotiation. The change is persisted instantly — no Save button.

Two ways to mark Won

Drag the card into the Won column, OR click Mark as Won on the card. Both paths trigger the same cross-service promotion logic — CRM checks the destination stage’s type, and if it is won, fires the request to Accounts. Lost works the same way (closes the deal but no Accounts call).

9
Session G · the cross-service moment

Closing the deal — promotion to Accounts

Drag the Northwind card from Negotiation into Won. To the salesperson, the only visible thing is a “Deal stage updated” toast and the card landing in the Won column. Behind the scenes a chain of events fires.

9.1 What just happened across the wire

  1. CRM updated the deal’s stage_id to the Won stage and set actual_close_date to today.
  2. Because the new stage is type won, CRM called TryPromoteCompanyToAccountsAsync(company.id).
  3. That helper checked — via gRPC — whether Accounts already had a customer with id e5879ca3-… (Northwind’s CRM company id). It did not.
  4. So CRM submitted a client_vendor_request to Accounts, explicitly passing client_id = company.id so the same UUID flows through.
  5. Accounts inserted a row in client_vendor_requests with status = pending, requested_from_service = "CRM", and the same id e5879ca3-…. A 48-hour SLA timer started.

None of this blocked the salesperson’s win. The promotion is best-effort — if Accounts is down, the deal still goes Won; the promotion is logged as a non-blocking warning.

9.2 Finance reviews the request

Switch to Accounts → Vendors & Customers. The sidebar shows a 1 badge next to Pending Customers — that’s our Northwind request waiting for review.

Click the green check. Accounts opens an Approve Request modal pre-filled with everything CRM sent. The one field that’s blank is Tax ID (GST/PAN) — that’s a Finance concern that CRM never collected. Fill it in (we use 06AABCN1234E1ZF) and click Approve & Create.

9.3 The customer appears in the books

Behind the modal, Accounts:

  1. Updated the request’s status to approved.
  2. Auto-generated the next customer code (CUST-0001).
  3. Inserted a row into customers using request.id as the customer id — which equals the original CRM company.id from step 4 above. Same UUID throughout.

9.4 Round-trip closes in CRM

Switch back to CRM → Companies. Northwind’s Status badge has flipped from neutral PROSPECT to green CUSTOMER. The flip happens automatically the next time the CRM page reads its companies list — CRM cross-checks Accounts for any matching customer ids and renders the badge accordingly.

What just happened across the database

Three rows now share the UUID e5879ca3-…: the CRM companies row, the Accounts client_vendor_requests row (now status=approved), and the Accounts customers row. Any future deal, proforma, or invoice in either system can FK back to this single identity. Sales and Finance see the same counterparty without ever talking about “the customer record”.

Reference

Glossary

TermMeaning
PipelineThe set of deal stages. A tenant can have many pipelines (e.g. New Business, Renewals); the default one is “Default”.
StageOne column on the kanban. Has a stage_type (open/won/lost) and a win_probability percent used in forecast totals.
Lead SourceA configured channel through which leads arrive (Website Contact Form, Google Ads, LinkedIn, etc.). Each gets a webhook URL for inbound automation.
LeadAn unqualified person of interest. Has its own status track (new → contacted → qualified → converted | unqualified) and converts into a Contact + Deal.
CompanyAn organisation in the CRM. Local-only by default. Status is Prospect until promoted to an Accounts customer.
ContactA person inside a Company. Holds the relationship history.
DealA revenue opportunity for a Company. Lives on the kanban; has a value, expected close date, and stage.
PromotionThe cross-service event fired when a deal moves to a stage of type won. CRM submits a client_vendor_request to Accounts.
CustomerA Company that has been promoted and approved in Accounts. Same UUID as the CRM company. Eligible for invoicing.
client_vendor_requestThe Accounts-side row created by promotion. Has status pending / approved / rejected and a 48-hour SLA. Approval generates the customer (or vendor) record.
CUST-NNNNThe auto-generated customer code in Accounts (or VEND-NNNN for vendors). Tenant-scoped monotonic.
