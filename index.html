The part nobody else has

All eleven, operable by conversation.

Every module publishes its actions to an agent as tools — over a thousand of them, and roughly half change something. Raise the invoice, approve the leave, add the vendor, score the call. It calls the same endpoints the screens call, so it can only ever do what your own role already permits.

It has no privileges you do notThe tool list is cut to your licensed modules and your roles before the model sees it, and calls carry your own token to the module that owns the data.
Nothing is written without youWrites are intercepted and previewed, and nothing changes until you confirm. Afterwards it re-reads the record to prove the change landed.
And it does real jobs, not just lookupsAn analyst that runs cross-tabs and key drivers over your survey data. A copilot that puts objections in front of the host mid-call. Calls scored against your own playbook. A WhatsApp agent blocked from stating a number that is not in your knowledge base.

The word

What a business OS actually means.

It is not a grander word for ERP. The two cover different halves of a company, and almost every business ends up buying one of each and paying somebody to staple them together.

ERP

The back office, on one data model

Books, stock, procurement, manufacturing, payroll. Integrated with each other and genuinely well — this is a solved problem, and Odoo, ERPNext and Dynamics all solve it.

Ledger · stock · payroll No mailbox · no calls · no chat · no files
Productivity suite

The front office, on one data model

Mail, meetings, chat, files. Also integrated with each other, also well — Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace have done this for twenty years.

Mail · calls · chat · files No ledger · no stock · no payroll
Business OS

Both halves, on one data model

The mail that started the order and the journal entry that ends it are rows in the same system, under the same permissions, on the same customer. There is no join to maintain because there is no join.

Mail · calls · chat · files · ledger · stock · payroll Eleven modules — about twenty-eight products' worth
