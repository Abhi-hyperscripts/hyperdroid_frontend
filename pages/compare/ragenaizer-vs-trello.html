Being straight

When you should stay where you are.

A comparison page that finds no reason to keep the other tool is not a comparison, it is an advert. Three honest ones.

You only need this one thing

If Trello is the only software your business runs and you have no intention of adding a CRM, projects or HR, a specialist tool will go deeper in its own lane than we will. Our argument is the stack, not the single app.

Your team is fluent in it

Retraining has a real cost, and it lands on the people who are busiest. If everyone is fast in Trello and nothing is actually breaking, the licence saving may not cover the disruption this year.

You depend on something we lack

Check the table below against the handful of things you genuinely could not work without. If one of them is missing here, that is a reason to wait — ask us and we will tell you honestly whether it is on the way.

What leaves your stack

The things you stop running.

Not features you gain — work you stop doing. That is usually where the real saving is, and it never appears on a licence invoice.

The Power-Up Tax

Time tracking? Power-up. Calendar? Power-up. Reporting? Power-up. Custom fields? Power-up. The "free" Trello becomes paid the moment you need anything beyond a card and a column.

Ragenaizer: every feature included on every plan

Flat Board Hierarchy

Every project is its own board. Want sub-projects? Make another board. Want a portfolio view? Power-up. There's no real hierarchy, just a list of boards and a folder you can drop them in.

Ragenaizer: Client → Project → Sub-Project hierarchy

No Time Tracking, No Billing

Trello cards have a due date and that's it. No hours logged, no billable rates, no client invoices. Agencies and consultancies always end up running Trello plus Harvest plus QuickBooks.
