Procurement / Ranking Methodology

How We Rank & Recommend Vendors

A transparent look at our two-phase vendor evaluation — from choosing who to invite, to selecting the winner after quotes arrive.

1

Two Phases, Three Layers Overview

Vendor evaluation happens at two distinct moments in the procurement lifecycle. Each phase uses different data and different methods.

Phase 1

Before quotes arrive. AI suggests which vendors to invite based on history & performance.

Phase 2

After quotes are received. Three layers analyze the quotes: math, scoring, and AI insights.

Your Decision

Always human. Three recommendations inform your judgment — they never replace it.

Phase 1
Before RFQ is sent — Who should we invite?

AI Vendor Suggestions Phase 1 · Choosing Which Vendors to Invite

Before quotes come in, you need to decide which vendors to invite. The "AI Suggest Vendors" button on an RFQ uses AI to analyze your procurement history and recommend the best candidates for each item.

1

Data Gathering

For every active vendor in your system, we collect a performance profile:

  • Selection history: How many times was this vendor chosen when they participated in past RFQs? Vendors you consistently select rank higher.
  • Quality rating: Average quality score (1–5 stars) from past transaction reviews and goods inspections.
  • Average pricing: Historical average quoted price across past quotes — gives the AI a sense of where they sit in the market.
  • Category count: How many item categories they supply — indicates breadth of expertise.
2

AI Analysis

The AI receives three inputs and produces per-item vendor recommendations:

INPUT 1: Items

Each RFQ item with name, quantity, unit, specifications, and master catalog ID.

INPUT 2: Vendor Profiles

All active vendors with quality scores, selection history, avg pricing, and categories.

INPUT 3: Memories

Your organization's procurement preferences, vendor rejections, and documented decision patterns.

The AI evaluates each vendor against each item and returns a confidence score (0–100%) plus 3–4 concise reasoning bullets for every recommendation.

3

Per-Item Ranking

For each item in the RFQ, the AI ranks vendors independently. A vendor that's great for towels may not be recommended for kitchen equipment — each item gets its own ranked list.

Per-item output: Vendor A → 95% confidence, Vendor B → 78% confidence, Vendor C → 62% confidence
Each vendor gets concise reasoning: "Selected 8 of 10 times when quoted", "Avg quality 4.8/5.0", etc.
4

Overall Ranking (Aggregation)

The "Overall Ranking" view aggregates across all items. This is done on the frontend, not by AI:

Average Confidence = Σ(confidence per item) ÷ number of items recommended for
Tiebreaker: Number of items where this vendor was #1 pick (topPicks)
Overall Rank: Sort by Average Confidence desc, then topPicks desc
Example

RFQ has 3 items: Towels, Bedsheets, Pillows.
Vendor A: Towels 95%, Bedsheets 88%, Pillows 92% → Avg 91.7%, 2 top picks
Vendor B: Towels 78%, Bedsheets 95%, Pillows 70% → Avg 81.0%, 1 top pick
→ Vendor A ranks #1 overall. Shown as "Recommended for 3/3 items, 91% avg confidence."

You can switch between "Overall Ranking" and per-item views in the suggestions modal. The suggestions are cached per RFQ and can be regenerated.

This phase answers: "Based on our history and AI analysis, which vendors should we invite to quote?"
You can accept the suggestions, modify them, or add vendors manually — the AI informs, you decide.
Phase 2
After quotes received — Which vendor should we select?
1

Layer 1 — Deterministic Price Comparison Step 1 · Pure Math · No AI

The first layer is completely objective. No weights, no history, no opinions — just arithmetic.

  • Item-by-item collection: For every item in the RFQ, we collect each vendor's quoted unit price.
  • Total calculation: Each vendor's total = sum of (unit price × quantity) for all items.
  • Cheapest vendor: The vendor with the lowest total across all items.
  • Per-item tracking: We also track which vendor is cheapest on each individual item — a vendor might win overall but be expensive on specific items.
Vendor Total = Σ (unit_pricei × quantityi)  for all items i
Cheapest Vendor = min(Vendor Total) across all vendors

This layer answers: "Who is the cheapest?" — nothing more, nothing less. Zero subjectivity.

2

Layer 2 — Heuristic Weighted Scoring Step 2 · Multi-Factor Analysis · No AI

The cheapest vendor isn't always the best. Layer 2 combines price, quality, and delivery into a single weighted score. All weights are configurable by your admin.

50%
Price Score
Linear scale 0–100. Cheapest vendor = 100, most expensive = 0. Interpolated for everyone in between.
30%
Quality Score
From historical vendor performance (1–5 stars → 0–100). New vendors default to 50 — no penalty, no advantage.
20%
Delivery Score
100 for on-time delivery, minus 10 per day late. Floor at 0. Early delivery = 100.
Weighted Total = (Price Score × 0.50) + (Quality Score × 0.30) + (Delivery Score × 0.20)
Best Value Vendor = highest Weighted Total
Example Calculation

Vendor A: Price 78 × 0.50 + Quality 92 × 0.30 + Delivery 100 × 0.20 = 86.6
Vendor B: Price 100 × 0.50 + Quality 50 × 0.30 + Delivery 80 × 0.20 = 81.0
→ Vendor A wins on value despite costing more, because of superior quality and delivery.

This layer answers: "Who offers the best overall value?" — price matters most (50%), but reliability and quality count too.

3

Layer 3 — AI Qualitative Analysis Step 3 · Expert Insights · With AI

Numbers tell you what. AI tells you why. When you click "AI Analysis" on a comparison, the AI receives all quote data and vendor history, then provides:

  • Summary analysis: A human-readable overview of the competitive landscape.
  • Per-vendor quality assessment: What do the prices suggest about material quality, construction, and brand positioning?
  • Price justification: Why one vendor charges more — premium materials, certifications, warranty terms, etc.
  • Contextual recommendation: Which vendor fits your specific use case (luxury hotel vs. budget property, etc.).
Key principle: AI never decides alone. It always supplements the heuristic layer.
The AI may recommend a different vendor than the cheapest or best-value — and that's the point. It adds context that numbers cannot capture.

AI insights are cached per comparison and can be regenerated on demand. They help you justify procurement decisions to stakeholders.

4

Confidence Score Step 4 · Data Quality Indicator

Every comparison shows a confidence percentage. This tells you how much data was available to make the ranking — not how certain the result is, but how complete the inputs are.

80–100%: High confidence — comprehensive data.
50–80%: Moderate — some gaps in history.
Below 50%: Low — consider gathering more quotes or performance data.

5

Three Recommendations Step 5 · The Final Output

Every comparison produces up to three independent recommendations — one from each layer. When they agree, you can be confident. When they differ, that's where the real insight lives.

Cheapest

Lowest Price

From Layer 1 (deterministic). The vendor with the lowest total quote. No weighting, no history — just arithmetic.

Best Value

Highest Weighted Score

From Layer 2 (heuristic). Balances price (50%), quality (30%), and delivery (20%). May differ from cheapest.

AI Recommended

Contextual Best Fit

From Layer 3 (AI). Considers your specific use case, material quality, brand positioning, and risk factors.

When all three agree: Strong signal — the choice is clear.
When they differ: The system is surfacing a nuance. The cheapest might have quality concerns. The best-value might not fit a luxury context. The AI pick might cost more but last longer. That tension is the value of having three layers.

Learning Loop
How the system gets smarter over time

The Continuous Learning Loop How Every Decision Makes the System Smarter

This is our most important feature. Every procurement cycle — from sending an RFQ to receiving goods — feeds data back into the system. The AI doesn't just make one-time recommendations; it learns from every outcome to improve future suggestions.

1

Quote Capture

When: A vendor submits a quote for an RFQ.
What's recorded: Every quoted price for every item is permanently stored in the vendor's history. Over time, this builds a comprehensive pricing database — the system learns what each vendor typically charges for each type of item, and how their pricing trends over months.

2

Decision Recording

When: You select a vendor from the comparison.
What's recorded: The chosen vendor is marked as the winner, and all other participating vendors are marked as not selected — for every item in the RFQ. This creates a win/loss record per vendor per item. The system also records whether you followed the AI recommendation, chose manually, or overrode the AI — and why.

Selection Rate = times won ÷ times quoted
A vendor that was chosen 8 out of 10 times they quoted has an 80% selection rate — a strong signal for future recommendations.
3

Delivery Tracking

When: A purchase order is fulfilled and goods arrive.
What's recorded: The promised delivery timeline vs. the actual arrival date. The system automatically calculates whether the vendor was early, on time, or late — and by how many days. This directly feeds the Delivery Score used in Phase 2's heuristic scoring.

4

Quality Inspection

When: Goods are inspected upon receipt.
What's recorded: The number of items ordered vs. items rejected, which the system uses to automatically compute a rejection percentage. Additionally, inspectors can add manual quality ratings (1–5 stars) with notes. All of this feeds the Quality Score in heuristic scoring.

5

AI Memory Formation

When: Admins explicitly teach the system, or patterns emerge from accumulated data.
What's stored: Six types of structured knowledge that the AI references when making recommendations:

Vendor Preferences
"For electronics, prefer TechSupplies Inc — consistent delivery, quality >4.5"
Vendor Rejections
"Avoid BadSteel Co — late 3/5 times, quality score 2.1"
Pricing Expectations
"Chemicals should average ₹320/unit, max ₹450"
Decision Patterns
"For electrical components, quality (50%) over price (30%)"
Quality Notes
"ISO certification required for food-grade packaging"
Item Preferences
"600GSM towels only — 400GSM rejected by guest feedback"

Each memory carries a confidence level indicating how reliable it is, a source showing where it came from (admin input, AI-derived pattern, learned from usage, or user feedback), and a usage count tracking how often the AI has referenced it in past recommendations. Stale memories that haven't been used gradually lose influence.

6

Next Cycle Benefits

When: The next RFQ is created for similar items.
What happens: All accumulated data flows back into both phases:

  • Phase 1 (Vendor Suggestions): AI sees vendor win rates, quality scores, pricing history, preferences, rejections, and decision patterns — all from past cycles.
  • Phase 2 (Quote Comparison): Heuristic scoring uses updated quality scores, delivery variance, and rejection rates. AI analysis references accumulated memories for context.
The result: A vendor that delivered late and had quality issues last month will automatically score lower in the next comparison — without anyone manually adjusting weights. The system learns from outcomes, not just inputs.
