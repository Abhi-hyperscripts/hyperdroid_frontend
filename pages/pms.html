What it covers

Client, project, task, issue, hour.

One hierarchy the whole delivery side hangs off, so a question about a client and a question about a bug are answered from the same place.

Clients & projects

Clients with several contacts, projects beneath them and sub-projects beneath those — one level of nesting, deliberately, because two is where structure becomes admin.

Issue tracker

Bugs, features, tasks and regressions with their own numbered key per project, severity, priority, component, environment and reproducibility.

Issue board

A board grouped by state, a personal "my issues" view, and bulk update for the afternoon somebody triages forty of them at once.

Threaded comments

Replies nest, so a long argument about one line of a bug does not flatten into a wall nobody can follow six weeks later.

Attachments

Screenshots and logs on the issue and on individual comments — because "it looks like this" is usually faster than describing it.

Watchers

Follow an issue without owning it, so the person who reported it and the person who cares about it both hear when it moves.

Issue history

Every status change, reassignment and edit kept, so "when did this become critical, and who said so" has an answer.

Time tracking

Hours and minutes against a task or a project, entered daily or in one bulk pass at the end of the week when nobody did it daily.

Weekly timesheets

A week submitted as one thing, reviewed from a pending queue, approved or sent back. Chasing timesheets stops being somebody's Monday.

Billing rates

A rate per member per project, snapshotted onto each entry as it is logged — so changing somebody's rate never rewrites what you already billed.

Project rooms

Each project gets a meeting room in the same platform, so the stand-up is not a link somebody pastes into chat every morning.

Tags & activity

Labels across projects, tasks and issues, and an activity feed of what actually changed rather than what somebody remembered to announce.

Straight answers

The things people ask on the first call.

01

Is the issue tracker a real one?

Yes. Numbered keys per project, issue types, severity, priority, component, environment, reproducibility, resolutions, duplicate linking, threaded comments, attachments, watchers and full history.

And the three fields that make a report useful — steps, expected and actual — are required, not optional boxes somebody skips at five to six.

02

Do we need a separate bug tracker?

That is the point of it being here. Delivery teams usually run a project tool and a bug tracker and reconcile them by hand; an issue and the task it came from sit in the same hierarchy under the same client.

03

How is average resolution time worked out?

From timestamps the system records itself — first response, resolved, closed — not from anyone remembering to start and stop a clock. The same stamps give you the breakdowns by severity, priority, component and resolution.

04

What if somebody's rate changes mid-project?

The rate is captured onto each time entry as it is logged. Change a rate today and everything already billed stays as it was billed — a retroactive change cannot quietly rewrite last quarter's revenue.

05

How do timesheets get approved?

A week is submitted as a single timesheet and lands in a pending queue for review, where it is approved or sent back. It is not an email thread, and it is not forty separate entries somebody ticks off.

06

Can we bill from it?

Approved hours carry their snapshotted rate into a billable amount, and the invoice is raised in Accounts against the same client record — not a lookalike somebody created with a slightly different spelling.

07

How deep does the structure go?

Client, project, sub-project — and no further, on purpose. One level of nesting covers how delivery work is actually organised; two is where a structure starts costing more to maintain than it explains.

08

Who can see what?

Project membership decides it. Members see and log against their projects; administrators see across them. Creating an issue requires being on the project, so the tracker does not fill up with reports from people with no context.
