The difference

They sell, then you reconcile.

On the usual setup the store is one system and the books are another. You bolt on a tax app, export to Tally, and count stock by hand to find out which of the two was right. Here the storefront is the accounting system, which is the one thing that cannot be retrofitted onto an incumbent.

An order is a ledger entryEvery sale writes a GST-correct invoice and a balanced general-ledger entry as it happens. Not an export, not a nightly sync, not a spreadsheet someone reconciles on Monday.
Stock that cannot driftThe catalogue, the prices and the stock are the ones in your books, costed FIFO. Selling from the storefront and selling from the counter move the same numbers, because there is only one set.
Offers that post correctlyCoupons with caps and expiry, automatic discounts scoped to a customer segment, buy-X-get-Y and free shipping — each one posting through the same single ledger entry rather than being typed over a total.
Gift cards as a real liabilityAn unredeemed gift card is money you owe, so it sits on the balance sheet as a liability and comes off when it is spent. Most storefronts treat it as a discount code and quietly understate what the business owes.
Returns that produce a credit noteAn RMA runs the return through the books properly — a credit note against the invoice, stock back where it came from, tax reversed. Not a refund somebody remembers to record.
Subscriptions and digital goodsRecurring orders bill on a schedule, and a digital product or a course is delivered as access with a link and instructions rather than as a shipment — checkout skips the address entirely.
The carts people abandonRecovery for the ones that stall at checkout, wishlists and reviews for the ones that do not, and Meta Pixel, CAPI and UTM capture so the ad spend can be judged against orders rather than clicks.
Thirty looks, ten tradesThirty theme presets across grocery, fashion, electronics, beauty, luxury, wellness, furniture, food, FMCG and general retail — built on four different homepage structures, so a grocery store is laid out like a grocery store rather than a recoloured fashion site.
Installs like an app, without being oneCustomers add it to the home screen on Android or iOS straight from the browser. There is no store listing, no review queue and no release to ship — and the installed icon, name and splash screen carry your brand, because the manifest is generated from your theme.
Your infrastructure, your domainYou host it. The code is yours to run and to change, on a server you control, behind a domain you own — with the API key and every payment and shipping secret encrypted at rest on your side.

If your business needs those, an incumbent is the honest answer today.

Straight answers

The things a merchant asks first.

01

What does it cost?

The storefront is free and you host it yourself. There is no transaction fee, no per-app subscription and nothing metered — the parts that are usually sold as add-ons elsewhere, like returns, subscriptions and gift cards, are simply in it. What you need is a Ragenaizer licence, because the books it posts into are the Accounts module.

02

How does it connect to my books?

One storefront API key, generated in Ragenaizer under Settings. The storefront backend holds it and calls Ragenaizer over the network; the browser never sees it, and it is stored encrypted along with your payment and shipping secrets. Point it at your tenant and the catalogue, stock and prices are the ones your accounts already have.

03

What does "accounting-native" actually mean?

That an order is not an event you reconcile later. Placing one writes a GST-correct invoice and a balanced general-ledger entry, moves stock on FIFO, and — for a return — issues a credit note rather than an informal refund note. A gift card is carried as a liability until it is redeemed, because that is what it is. There is no export, no nightly sync and no second system holding a different number.

04

Is it really thirty themes, or one theme in thirty colours?

Thirty presets across ten retail verticals, built on four genuinely different homepage structures — an editorial split hero, a full-bleed spotlight, a search-first market grid, and a deals layout with a promo rail. Grid density, card shape and typography change with the structure, not just the palette. What it is not is a drag-and-drop builder; you are picking and tuning a preset.

05

How does the phone app work?

It is a progressive web app, so a customer installs it from the browser on Android or iOS and there is no app-store build, review or release to manage. The manifest is generated from your theme, so the icon, name, splash and colours are yours — the installed app is your brand, not ours.

06

Where does it fall short of Shopify?

Breadth, and we would rather say it here than let you find out later. There is no app marketplace, no multi-channel selling into marketplaces and social, and no email-campaign suite. Payments are Razorpay today. Tax is Indian GST. Those are real gaps for some businesses and none at all for others, which is why the table above marks them rather than omitting them.

07

Can I change the code?

Yes — it is yours to run and yours to modify, on your own infrastructure and your own domain. The trade for that is that you are running it: the hosting, the updates and the backups are on your side of the line.
